Pyrite looks forward to working with the security community to identify vulnerabilities in the Pyrite website and our open-source projects to help ensure the security of our business, our users, and the broader ecosystem.

## Code of Conduct

Please only test assets listed as in scope. Reports concerning issues affecting other URLs will likely be closed as out of scope.

Throughout your research, you must make every effort to avoid violating privacy, destroying data, or disrupting or degrading our services. Please only interact with accounts you own, or with accounts for which you have obtained explicit permission from the account holder.

When conducting testing, we ask that you follow these guidelines:

- Submit one vulnerability per report, unless you need to chain multiple vulnerabilities together to demonstrate their impact;
- In the event of duplicate reports, we will only accept the first report received, provided that the issue can be fully reproduced;
- Multiple vulnerabilities resulting from the same underlying issue will be evaluated at our discretion;
- Reports that prompt us to update our documentation to describe security risks will be evaluated at our discretion;
- Reports concerning publicly disclosed zero-day vulnerabilities for which an official patch has been available for fewer than 7 days may be rewarded on a case-by-case basis;
- False positives and/or theoretical reports generated by automated scanners or artificial intelligence will, at our discretion, be closed as Not Applicable. You must validate all vulnerabilities and provide a working proof of concept (PoC) when submitting a report.

We also ask that you avoid:

- Denial-of-service (DoS) attacks;
- Spam;
- Social engineering attacks against Pyrite team members, including phishing;
- Any physical attacks against Pyrite property or data centers.

To help us investigate your findings and prevent your testing activity from being flagged and/or blocked by our security systems, please include the following HTTP headers when testing online assets owned by Pyrite:

- `X-HackerOne-Handle: <your-hackerone-username>`
- `X-HackerOne-Tool: <tool-used>`

If you need to register an account for testing purposes, please use your `@wearehackerone.com` email alias.

## Submission Requirements

We actively seek to work with researchers who identify genuinely impactful vulnerabilities and write clear, actionable reports. We always take reports seriously when they demonstrate that the findings are valid, provide a working exploit chain, and clearly articulate the impact. Report quality is essential: concise, well-supported reports with a convincing proof of concept (PoC) will always take priority over a high volume of reports.

Reports that have not been manually validated, appear to have been generated by artificial intelligence tools, contain false or unverified claims, or consist solely of scanner output without a valid exploit will be closed as Not Applicable. Researchers who submit such reports will be flagged and deprioritized within the program.

## Third-Party Dependencies

Pyrite uses numerous third-party dependencies and has forked some of them to make custom modifications. If the root cause of a vulnerability stems from Pyrite's use or modification of a dependency, the vulnerability is in scope for this program. Otherwise, please report it directly to the upstream maintainers of the affected dependency.

Please do not submit reports generated by automated scanners, especially reports concerning third-party dependencies. Unless you provide a working PoC demonstrating exploitability, such reports will be closed as Not Applicable.

## Supported Versions

Pyrite versions from the most recent release through the latest commit are in scope for this program. If an intermediate branch such as `develop` exists, the latest commit on that branch serves as the cutoff.

We make every effort to keep our dependencies, frameworks, and other third-party software up to date. However, reports concerning outdated dependencies or frameworks will, in most cases, be closed as Informative or rated Low severity.

## Disclosure

As an open-source project, we will make security fixes public as soon as possible and issue security advisories and/or CVE identifiers for serious vulnerabilities. We are generally happy to credit researchers in these advisories.

We fully support researchers' right to publicly disclose vulnerabilities they discover and will not attempt to prevent disclosures made in accordance with this policy. We only ask that you coordinate the timing of disclosure with us to avoid potential harm to our services, users, and other affected parties.

## Safe Harbor

For unintentional, good-faith violations of this policy, the Pyrite Project team and its members ("we") will not initiate civil action or file a complaint with law enforcement. If a third party initiates legal action against you and you have complied with this policy, we will take steps to make it clear that your activities were conducted in accordance with this policy. We consider activities conducted in compliance with this policy to constitute "authorized" activity under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA).
