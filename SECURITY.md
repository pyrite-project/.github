Pyrite looks forward to collaborating with the security community to identify vulnerabilities in the Pyrite website and our open-source projects to ensure the security of our business, our users, and the broader ecosystem.

## Code of Conduct

Please only test assets explicitly listed as in scope. Reports concerning issues on other URLs will likely be closed as out of scope.

Throughout your research, you must make every effort to avoid privacy violations, data destruction, and disruption or degradation of our services. Only interact with accounts you own or accounts for which you have obtained explicit permission from the account holder.

When conducting testing, we ask that you adhere to the following guidelines:

- Submit one vulnerability per report, unless you need to chain multiple vulnerabilities together to demonstrate their impact;
- In the event of duplicate reports, we will only accept the first report received, provided that the vulnerability can be fully reproduced;
- Multiple vulnerabilities resulting from the same underlying issue will be evaluated at our discretion;
- Reports that lead us to update our documentation to describe security risks will be evaluated at our discretion;
- Reports concerning publicly disclosed zero-day vulnerabilities for which an official patch was released less than 7 days ago will be considered for rewards on a case-by-case basis;
- False positives and/or theoretical reports generated by automated scanners or artificial intelligence will be marked as "Not Applicable" and closed at our discretion. You must validate all vulnerabilities and provide a working proof of concept when submitting a report.

We also ask that you avoid:

- Denial-of-service (DoS) attacks;
- Spam;
- Social engineering attacks against Pyrite team members, including phishing;
- Any physical attacks against Pyrite team property or data centers.

To help us investigate your findings and prevent your testing activities from being flagged and/or blocked by our security systems, please include the following HTTP headers when testing online assets owned by Pyrite:

- `X-HackerOne-Handle: <your-hackerone-username>`
- `X-HackerOne-Tool: <tool-used>`

If you need to register an account for testing purposes, please use your `@wearehackerone.com` email alias.

## Submission Requirements

We actively seek to collaborate with researchers who identify genuinely impactful vulnerabilities and write clear, actionable reports. We take reports seriously when they demonstrate the authenticity of the findings, provide a working exploit chain, and clearly explain the impact. Report quality is essential: concise, well-supported reports with strong proofs of concept (PoCs) are always preferred over a large quantity of reports.

Reports that have not been manually verified, appear to be generated by artificial intelligence tools, contain false or unsubstantiated claims, or consist solely of scanner output without a valid exploit demonstration will be marked as "Not Applicable." Researchers who submit such reports will be flagged and deprioritized within the program.

## Third-Party Dependencies

Pyrite uses numerous third-party dependencies, some of which we have forked to implement custom modifications. If the root cause of a vulnerability lies in Pyrite's use or modification of a dependency, the vulnerability is considered in scope for this program. Otherwise, please report the vulnerability directly to the upstream maintainers of the affected dependency.

Please do not submit reports generated by automated scanners, especially those concerning third-party dependencies. Reports that do not include a working proof of concept (PoC) demonstrating exploitability will be closed as "Not Applicable."

## Supported Versions

All versions of Pyrite, from the most recent release through the latest commit, are in scope for this program. If intermediate branches such as `develop` exist, the latest commit on those branches serves as the upper boundary of the supported range.

We strive to keep our dependencies, frameworks, and other third-party software up to date. However, reports concerning outdated dependencies or frameworks will generally be classified as Informational or Low severity.

## How to Submit

> *We are currently applying for HackerOne's Community Edition, in the meantime, please submit reports directly to the corresponding project's GitHub Private Vulnerability Reporting channel. If the project does not have Private Vulnerability Reporting enabled, you can also submit your report to the Pyrite-IDE project.*

## Disclosure

As an open-source project, we aim to publicly release security fixes as quickly as possible and issue security advisories and/or CVE identifiers for serious vulnerabilities. We are generally happy to acknowledge researchers for their contributions in these advisories.

We fully support researchers' right to publicly disclose vulnerabilities they discover and will not attempt to prevent disclosures made in accordance with this policy. We only ask that you coordinate the timing of disclosure with us to avoid potential harm to our services, users, and other affected parties.

## Safe Harbor

For accidental, good-faith violations of this policy, the Pyrite Project team and its members ("we") will not initiate civil lawsuits or file complaints with law enforcement. If a third party initiates legal action against you and you have complied with this policy, we will take steps to demonstrate that your activities were conducted in accordance with this policy. We consider all activities conducted in compliance with this policy to constitute "authorized" activity under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA).
